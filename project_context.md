# Project Context: Emergency Management Initiative in Maharashtra, India

## Geographical Context
- **Country**: India
- **State**: Maharashtra
- **District**: Thane (neighboring Mumbai)
- **Villages/Areas**: 38 villages/urban areas across 2 administrative zones
- **Population Covered**: Approximately 300,000 residents
- **Time Period**: 2012-2014

## Disaster Profile
Thane district in Maharashtra is highly vulnerable to multiple natural and man-made hazards, particularly monsoon-related floods, landslides, and building collapses. The district's rapid urbanization, informal settlements, and proximity to Mumbai have increased vulnerability to disasters. Historical data shows these areas experience major flooding events annually during monsoon seasons (June-September), with landslides in hilly areas and occasional building collapses in densely populated urban zones.

## Project Background
In 2012, the World Bank initiated a comprehensive emergency management program in Thane district following devastating floods that caused significant loss of life and property damage. Initial assessments revealed significant gaps in emergency service response times, community preparedness, coordination mechanisms, and response capabilities. The baseline data collected in 2012 showed concerning emergency service arrival times and low preparedness scores across all 38 target villages/urban areas.

## Intervention Approach
The project implemented the O2C3 protocol framework:
- **Objective-based**: Clear, measurable goals for each community
- **Operational level of detail**: Practical, actionable procedures
- **Consensus-based**: Inclusive decision-making with community leaders
- **Capability-based**: Tailored to local resources and skills
- **Compliant**: Aligned with national policies and cultural norms

This framework was operationalized through 20 community-led disaster drills conducted between 2012-2014, with comprehensive data collection before, during, and after each simulation.

## Measurement Methodology
- **Response Time**: Measured as emergency service arrival time from initial alert to arrival at affected site
- **Preparedness Scores**: Composite index (0-100) based on the City Resilience Index framework, focusing on infrastructure readiness, knowledge levels, coordination effectiveness, and resource availability
- **Community Engagement**: Participation rates in drills and training sessions
